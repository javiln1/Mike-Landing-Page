# RSA Ad Script Audit

**Client:** Mike, Remote Sales Academy (RSA)
**Date:** 2026-10-27
**Scope:** the 31 old Meta talking-head scripts in `Scripts-Database/mike-scripts.json`. These are the 11 tested scripts (001–010 and 016) and the 20 untested ones (017–036). They are checked against Jeremy Haynes (Messaging Mastery 01–04, the Master Messaging SOP, Direct Response Ad Mastery, Majority Hook Mastery, the DR Ad Creation Framework SOP and parts of Facebook Ads Mastery), Schwartz's *Breakthrough Advertising*, the School of Yalla Round 1 and Round 3 style, and the live funnel.
**Funnel checked:** `design-options/option-1.html` (landing page), `design-options/shared.js` (application), `design-options/confirmation-page.html`, and the VSL. The VSL transcript is in [RSA VSL - Transcript.txt](RSA%20VSL%20-%20Transcript.txt).

**How to read the tags**

- **(S)** comes from the sources (Jeremy, Schwartz, the funnel files, the script database).
- **(I)** is reasoned inference. It is my judgment and not proven.
- **(T)** means only paid testing can settle it.

**A caveat on the evidence.** The "winner" and "top performer" labels come from a note in the JSON: bookings are "highest" or "moderate". There is no CPA, show rate, close rate or cash figure for any of them **(S)**. Those bookings also came through self-booking, which no longer exists. The KPI is now a qualified application that the team reaches within 12 hours. So "most bookings" proves the scripts got volume. It does not prove they got buyers **(I)**.

## 1. Verdict in 5 lines

1. **Why they worked:** they opened on a real, ordinary person with a clear before and after (bricklayer, call-centre worker, low-paid corporate job), used plain spoken rhythm, and reframed the problem as "the wrong vehicle, not you". That is Jeremy's "if that guy can do it, so can I" proof, and it landed in a less tired market **(S/I)**.
2. **What holds them back first:** none of them names the viewer's own situation or the moment it hurts. They sell somebody else's outcome, which is a Stage 2 claim in a market that is now at Stage 4–5 **(S/I)**.
3. **No mechanism:** the only "how" is "learn remote sales", "scripts" or "warm leads". The real differentiator in today's VSL is that Mike trains you for roles he already has and then places you, and it appears in none of the 31 scripts **(S)**.
4. **The bridge is broken in three places.** The ads say "free training… see you on the next page… no boss". The VSL is about a capped job, 90 days of training, placement, and a team call within 12 hours. The landing page targets people already in sales, while the VSL tells viewers that experienced salespeople are "the worst people I've ever trained" **(S)**.
5. **The claims are the biggest liability.** Louis's numbers are mathematically wrong in four scripts. Sam is £6K in the ads, $8K and $15K on the page. Zane is "$9K" in one place and "Zain, $12K" in another. None of it is substantiated well enough to defend on Meta **(S)**.

## 3. What the 5 top performers share, and what to keep

**What they share (S, from the scripts):**

- **A named person and a real job in the first sentence:** bricklayer, call centre, corporate Toronto. (009 and 016 are the exceptions.)
- **A concrete before and after with a timeline**, delivered within about 8 seconds.
- **The "not your fault" reframe:** "the problem wasn't his effort, it was the vehicle", "Louis wasn't broke, he was just stuck", "your job isn't to convince them".
- **One objection handled by explaining how the job really works** (016: you don't cold call, the leads come to you).
- **Short, spoken sentences** and a signature close: "Same Sam. Completely different life."
- **Identical CTAs:** "free training… click below and I'll see you on the next page." This is shared by all 31 scripts, so it cannot be why these five won **(S/I)**.

**Why they probably won (I):** in a broad market, proof-led stories pull the most volume. The bricklayer and call-centre worker read as people "in a worse position than me", and Jeremy calls that the strongest testimonial type **(S, Peak Trust)**. 016 removed the most common fear. 009 pulled dreamers. Volume isn't the same as quality, and we can't tell which of these produced buyers **(T)**.

**Keep:**

1. **Zane, Sam and Louis as proof**, but placed after the viewer's own pain, not instead of it. Sam is the strongest because of the "worse position than me" archetype.
2. **The condition lines from 003 and 008:** "You make good money… so why does it feel like you're still trapped?" and "He wasn't broke. He was just stuck." Both are the VSL's own condition **(S)**.
3. **The reframe "same work ethic, wrong vehicle".** It is the Yalla move ("it isn't your fault, it's a mismatch") in Mike's words.
4. **The warm-lead mechanism from 016.** Explain it in one line.
5. **Plain spoken delivery.** Numbers written out, short sentences, no lists.

**Drop:** the Bali lifestyle (009), "no boss / no commute / no ceiling" as the payoff, "free training… next page", and every Louis income line until Mike re-verifies it.

## 4. The biggest gaps against Jeremy, Schwartz and the Yalla style

Each gap has a one-line before/after. The after lines only show the direction; they are not the new scripts.

**Gap 1: the hook calls out a student, not the viewer (S: DR SOP "speak to real-time context"; Yalla Round 3).**
Jeremy: open on the viewer's circumstance, not a demographic line. Yalla opens on the viewer's own moment.

- Before: "This is Zane. Six months ago, he was stuck in a corporate job in Toronto…"
- After: "If you're the one at work everybody trusts with the difficult customers, and your pay hasn't moved in two years, this is for you."

**Gap 2: the pain is surface-level (S: Lesson 04, the iceberg; "freedom" is the tip).**
"Stuck", "trapped" and "freedom" are top-of-iceberg words. Jeremy wants a specific scene that "itches", with current or recurring pain first.

- Before: "Louis wasn't broke. He was just… stuck."
- After: "It's the pay review where you hit every target, get two percent again, and still say thank you."

**Gap 3: there is no mechanism, and placement is missing (S: Schwartz Stage 3, "a new mechanism"; the VSL).**
The VSL's mechanism is that the problem is access to roles, not the skill. Mike trains people for roles he already has and introduces them directly, because his reputation depends on them performing. No script says this.

- Before: "He just learned a high-income skill that most people don't even know exists."
- After: "Learning to close is the easy part. Getting a company to hire you is where most people get stuck, so we train you for roles we already have and introduce you directly."

**Gap 4: there's no reframe aimed at the viewer (Yalla).**
The old reframes are about the student. The Yalla structure turns it on the viewer: "it isn't a character flaw, it's a mismatch".

- Before: "The difference isn't how hard you work, it's WHAT you work on."
- After: "That's not a work-ethic problem. You're paid by the hour inside a pay band, and no amount of effort moves a pay band."

**Gap 5: the escape fantasy attracts the wrong condition (S: Lesson 01 Andrew/Amazon; SOP "conditions over demographics").**
Andrew's "quit your 9-5" ads pulled people who hate their life, not buyers. The VSL's best proof (Sultana kept teaching and added $5K a month) and its time pitch (two hours on Sunday, one after work on Monday) speak to someone who keeps their job **(S)**.

- Before: "No more commute. No more boss breathing down his neck."
- After: "You don't have to quit anything to start. Most people train in the evenings, around the job they already have."

**Gap 6: the CTA breaks the bridge (S: SOP Three-Part Bridge; VSL CTA).**
The VSL never calls itself a "free training" and ends with "fill in the application and we'll speak within 12 hours". The page does say "Watch the free training", so the "free training" wording isn't a hard mismatch. The real break is "the exact 3-step system… see you on the next page", which promises something the VSL doesn't deliver.

- Before: "I've put together a free training that shows you the exact 3-step system Zane used. Click below and I'll see you on the next page."
- After: "If that's your situation, click below and watch the short video. If it makes sense, apply, and the team will call you within 12 hours."

**Gap 7: in-market buyers are ignored (S: DR Ad Mastery and SOP, "in-market 3–4% first").**
Every old script is written to convince someone that remote sales exists. The VSL names a fully in-market person: someone who already paid for a closing course and is sending Looms to companies that never reply. That is exactly what Mike himself did for two years **(S)**. No script speaks to them.

- Before: "Then he discovered remote sales, a skill that pays you based on results, not hours."
- After: "If you've already done a closing course and you're still sending Looms to companies that never reply, the problem isn't your skill."

**Gap 8: no downsides, so no trust (S: Peak Trust, "too good to be true" is a grandfather belief; talking about downsides builds belief).**

- Before: "It really is that simple."
- After: "It's not quick money. It's ninety days of training and role-plays, and if we don't think you're ready, we'll tell you on the call."

**Gap 9: oversized or wrong numbers (S: SOP "the bigger the claim, the lower the quality"; Urgency, "the bigger the number, the less reality-based it is for regular people").**

- Before: "Now he makes £4K a week. That's more than his old annual salary, every single month."
- After: "Last year, over twenty of our students got to five thousand dollars a month or more. Results vary, and it takes work." (This is the VSL's own claim. Mike must prove it before it goes in an ad.)

**Gap 10: no urgency (S: Urgency lesson; it must be a believable reason, not "you don't get your time back").**
None of the scripts gives a believable reason to act now. The Yalla Round 3 version is the model: "another year of the same routine gets you another year of the same conversations".

- Before: (none)
- After: "Another year in the same pay band gets you another year of the same pay review."

**Gap 11: some scripts talk to more than one person (S: SOP "one coherent person in one condition"; "old men and schoolgirls").**
001 mixes the commuter, rent anxiety, envy of friends and Toronto. 009 mixes Bali, Barcelona and the couch. Each ad should hold one person in one condition.

## 6. Market awareness and sophistication (remote closing offers on Meta, 2026)

**Awareness (Schwartz, five levels):**

- **Career changers in a capped job** are mostly **problem-aware**: they feel the ceiling. They are partly **solution-aware**: they have seen "become a remote closer" ads for years **(I)**.
- **People who already bought a closing course and can't land a role** are **product-aware or most aware** of the category and sceptical of the next offer. This is Mike's own backstory **(S, VSL)**.
- **People already in sales** are **solution-aware**: they know high-ticket closing exists. They are not product-aware of RSA **(I)**.

**Sophistication: Stage 4 tipping into Stage 5 (I, strong):**

- The promise (close deals from your laptop, earn $10K a month) has been made and inflated since about 2020.
- The first mechanisms (scripts, warm leads, no cold calling) have been elaborated by many offers. That is Stage 4, where the market elaborates the mechanism **(S, Schwartz)**.
- The signs of Stage 5 fatigue are in the VSL itself. People have paid for courses, didn't get placed, and now ask "have I been scammed before?" and "what if I don't get placed?" **(S)**.
- Jeremy notes that course creators carry "the gnarliest reputation" with regular buyers **(S, Peak Trust)**.

**Mass desire to channel (Schwartz):** "Get paid for the people skills I already have, without gambling the stability I already have." It's not "escape to Bali" **(I)**.

**What that means for the hooks:**

1. **Lead with identification, not the promise.** At Stage 5 Schwartz says the headline should tell people what they are and "echo an emotion". That is the Yalla first line **(S)**.
2. **Bring in the one mechanism the market hasn't heard to death: placement.** The problem was never the skill, it's access to roles. That is the Stage 3 move of making an old promise believable again with a new "how" **(S)**.
3. **Use small, believable numbers and name the downsides.** At this stage, big numbers repel the buyers you want and attract the desperate **(S)**.
4. **Delete Stage 1 lines** like "a skill most people don't even know exists". In 2026 they flag the ad as dated or naive **(I)**.
5. **Open with a call-out of the condition, not the income.** Jeremy: "if you work a job and make more than $100K a year" makes people scroll; "you're still in that job and not getting paid nearly what you want to make" doesn't **(S, DR Ad Mastery)**. The VSL opens on an income line ("earning around $2,500 a month"). That's tolerable after the click, but the ads shouldn't copy it **(I)**.

## 7. Audience: salespeople, career changers, or both?

**This is the user's decision.** The evidence is below.

**For people already in sales (what the landing page targets) (S unless marked):**

- The page headline and title have targeted "9-5 salespeople" since the first funnel build on 2026-08-15.
- The application has a branch for current salespeople (SDR, B2B, B2C, closing, account management).
- James (appointment setter, $2K to $6.7K) is on the page.
- Jeremy says to go after the in-market group first. A working salesperson already believes in sales as a career, which matches the "already in real estate, already spending $70K" logic.
- **(I)** Placement partners want people who perform fast, and Mike's reputation depends on it. Salespeople lower that risk, and they usually earn more, so they can pay.
- **(I)** The 010 hook ("never wonder what to say on a call") hints at this lane.

**Against salespeople:**

- **The VSL actively repels them (S):** "the worst people I've ever trained are the ones who come in saying… I've got 10 plus years of experience… retraining someone takes longer than training someone from scratch."
- Every VSL story (Zane, Sultana, Masri) is a non-salesperson **(S)**.
- All of the ad evidence we have comes from career-changer scripts **(S)**.
- This lane is untested and a smaller pool on Meta **(I/T)**.
- Many 9-5 salespeople earn commission already, so the pay-band pain is weaker **(I)**.

**For career changers (employed, capped, not broke):**

- The old winners are the only data we have, though it's thin **(S)**.
- The VSL opens on exactly this person ("you've got a job… not starting from zero… the ceiling is there") **(S)**.
- Almost every proof card on the page fits: Zane, Sam, Kai, Sultana and Masri in the VSL **(S)**.
- The bricklayer and call-centre proof is Jeremy's strongest testimonial type **(S)**.
- The application already routes non-sales applicants **(S)**.

**Against career changers:**

- It's a needs-convinced pool with a "broke talk" risk **(S)**. The application even offers "$0" as an investment answer.
- It risks Andrew-style "quit my job" leads **(S)**.
- The old bookings happened under self-booking and may have been low quality **(T)**.
- The old proof stories may be fatigued after a year **(I)**.

**Both, as separate lanes:**

- Jeremy endorses separate in-market segments with their own hooks **(S, DR Ad Mastery)**.
- He also warns that one page talking to both is "old men and schoolgirls" **(S, SOP, Max's land offer)**.
- Today there is one page and one VSL, and the VSL contradicts the sales lane. The sales lane would need its own headline and a VSL cut first, which is funnel work **(I)**.
- At a modest budget, two lanes halve the learning speed **(I/T)**.

**Recommendation (I):**

- **Lead with employed career changers in a "capped, not broke" condition.** That means people in customer-facing jobs (call centre, customer service, retail, teaching, trades, junior corporate) who earn and keep their job while they train.
- **Run "already did a course, can't land a role" as a second hook family inside the same lane.** It's the most in-market group the VSL serves, and it carries the placement mechanism.
- **Hold "already in sales" for later**, as its own lane with its own page headline and an edited VSL intro.
- **Whichever audience is picked, one funnel fix is needed:** the landing page headline currently contradicts the VSL. That's a funnel change, which this audit doesn't make, and it needs Javi's and Mike's approval.

## 9. Claims guardrails for Meta

**Fix before any number goes in an ad (S):**

| Student | Ads say | Page / VSL say | Problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zane | $1,500 to $9,000/mo in 90 days, "6 months ago" | Page: $1,500 to $9K "verified role". Proof card: "Zain, $12K/mo, zero experience, closing in 5 weeks". VSL: $9K | Two spellings and two numbers. $1,500/mo full-time in Toronto is below minimum wage, so ask Mike what it was (part-time? another currency?). |
| Sam | £3K to £6K/mo, 60-hour weeks | Page: "$8K/mo", "inconsistent bricklaying work". Proof card: "$15K/mo" | Three numbers and two currencies. "60-hour weeks" vs "inconsistent work" contradict each other. |
| Louis | £24K/yr to £4K/week; "more than his old annual salary every month" | Not on the page or in the VSL | The maths is false (£4K/week ≈ £17K/month). The implied ~£200K/yr is the most extreme claim in the set. There's no funnel proof, so an ad with Louis brings in a claim the funnel never repeats. |

**Rules for the new batch:**

1. **Only use numbers Mike can prove with documents** (payment screenshots, contracts). Use one currency per ad, matched to the landing page ($ today) **(I)**.
2. **Prefer milestone claims over peak claims** (Jeremy, milestone outcomes). For example: "over twenty students passed five thousand a month in 2025". Keep that VSL claim only if Mike can substantiate it **(S)**.
3. **Never present a result as typical.** Say "results vary", show the effort ("ninety days of training", "role-plays") and keep the page disclaimer. US: the FTC endorsement guides require clear disclosure of typical results when a testimonial is atypical.
